# Graphics options and URP visual study

The Bootstrap Mac build uses a clean gameplay view for the KayKit art study. The upper-left resource counters, upper-right look and controls label, function-key shortcuts, and patterned spotlight are inactive. Open **Options → Graphics** from the title or Esc pause menu. The lower gameplay prompt, title-card controls footer, and ground decals have been removed. The status prompt remains available for save errors.

Combat feedback is on by default: dodges emit soft dust puffs, sword and axe swings draw a short weapon-tip trail with glints timed to the moving blade, and damaging hits emit warm sparks. These are presentation only and do not change combat timing or damage. Their generated textures use Unity's Particle System and Trail Renderer with an authored URP particle material; no external art or extra package is required.

The Graphics page contains five controls, ordered by expected frequency of change: **Camera Zoom** (Close, Balanced, Far), **Anti-aliasing** (Off, FXAA, SMAA, TAA, MSAA 2×/4×/8×), **Depth of Field** (Off, Gaussian, Bokeh), **Bloom** (on/off), and **Ambient Occlusion** (on/off). Depth of Field needs one dropdown: Off already performs the job of an on/off toggle. MSAA choices are exclusive with TAA. Display mode and window size stay on the parent Options page. Changes apply and save immediately; **Reset to Default** restores these five controls and saves them. Graphics choices use `visual-study-settings.json` in Unity's persistent data directory, separate from the gameplay save. Old selections load; historical Gaussian and Bokeh presets migrate when they still match their former defaults. Editor tests use isolated temporary settings paths.

## Current review defaults

The selected color and light defaults remain temperature **25**, exposure **0**, contrast **0**, saturation **10**, bloom strength **1.00** with threshold **0.70**, vignette **0**, home light **10**, home warmth **35**, shadow strength **0.90**, and **TAA**. Unity's positive temperature values add warmth; the local home grade adds another 35. Ambient occlusion begins on. These are study defaults, not established performance targets. Biomes may later have separate color temperature and atmosphere profiles.

The recommended gameplay focus mode remains **Gaussian**, with **Start 30**, **End 40**, and **Max Radius 0.5**. The player is about 22 world units from the camera, and the visible ground spans roughly 14–33 at the balanced zoom, so this leaves the player and most combat space sharp while adding only gentle far-edge softness. The previous 24/30/1 setting reached full blur on visible ground and could soften distant enemies. Unity calls Gaussian the faster but more limited mode and warns that radii above 1 can cause undersampling artifacts. [Unity depth-of-field reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/depth-of-field-volume-override-reference.html)

The Bokeh preference uses **Focus Distance 22**, **Aperture f/2.8**, **Focal Length 120 mm**, and **6 rounded blades**. The old f/1.25, 145 mm preset produced conspicuous near and far blur. Using URP's circle-of-confusion formula, the new preset reduces that strength to about 31% at the same depth; at the balanced camera's near/far ground distances, the nominal blur radius is roughly 1.9/1.1 pixels at 1080p, versus 6.2/3.6 pixels before. These are model estimates, not measured screen-space results. Bokeh still uses a 42-sample blur and can soften near-field objects, so Gaussian remains the default; players who prefer the lens-like result can choose Bokeh. [Unity Bokeh controls](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/depth-of-field-volume-override-reference.html)

The scene uses linear fog beginning at **26** world units. **Fog End 65** means the fog color would be fully applied at 65; a smaller end value strengthens haze in the visible far field, while a larger one weakens it. It is intentionally subtle at the current camera scale. [Unity fog start](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/ScriptReference/RenderSettings-fogStartDistance.html) · [fog end](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/ScriptReference/RenderSettings-fogEndDistance.html)

## Native Unity setup

- The [URP Volume framework](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/volumes-landing-page.html) uses `Painterly Clear` globally, `Warm Home` locally with the player as the camera's volume trigger, and a `Focus Preview` overlay. Authored profiles live under `Assets/Topaz/Presentation/Graphics/Profiles/`; the player clones them before applying saved menu choices.
- The default grade uses Neutral tonemapping to retain KayKit's gradient-atlas colors, the chosen warm color values, bloom, and Gaussian depth of field. [Bloom reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/post-processing-bloom.html)
- The camera defaults to TAA. It can ghost on fast motion, so SMAA remains an option. The Graphics menu also offers MSAA 2×/4×/8× as separate choices; selecting one disables camera post-process AA and changes the desktop URP asset's sample count at runtime. TAA and MSAA cannot be combined. [Unity 6.6 URP anti-aliasing](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/anti-aliasing.html)
- The camera's native **Dithering** option is on to reduce 8-bit banding across the floor's broad light and fog gradients. URP exposes this camera setting as on/off; it has no strength or pattern slider. [Unity camera reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/camera-component-reference.html)
- The desktop URP asset uses **64-bit HDR color buffer precision** (RGBA16F) for lighting and post-processing gradients, as requested. Unity notes that this can reduce banding but uses more memory bandwidth and can sample more slowly than 32-bit HDR; compare frame time once a representative scene exists. Final display dithering remains useful because it addresses the later 8-bit output stage. [Unity URP HDR precision](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/universalrp-asset.html)
- Standalone Player Settings use **High Quality lightmap encoding** for Mac and Windows. This governs baked lightmaps when Topaz generates them; the present test scene primarily uses real-time lighting, so this setting is unlikely to alter its current gradients. [Unity macOS Player settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/PlayerSettings-macOS.html)
- One real-time directional light changes color and strength with the [day and night cycle](../DAY_NIGHT_CYCLE.md); one unshadowed warm point light and a local Volume distinguish the homestead. The directional shadow distance is **52** world units with two cascades split at **45%**. The original 32-unit limit placed URP's final shadow fade inside the visible ground, so turning the aim or moving the camera could sweep the fade across the scene. At maximum zoom, the far visible ground is roughly 40 units from the camera. URP starts fading the new 52-unit shadows around 46 units, leaving room for current off-screen casters whose shadows reach the view. Recheck this margin when taller authored scenery arrives. A KayKit lantern has an emissive ember and a bounded built-in Particle System. [Unity URP shadow settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/universalrp-asset.html)
- The review build disables URP's **Strip Unused Post Processing Variants** setting. A headless build classified Gaussian depth of field as inactive and removed its shader, so runtime changes had no visible effect. Unity documents this setting as assuming that runtime Volume profiles do not introduce new effects; Topaz clones and changes Volume profiles for Graphics options. Re-evaluate targeted stripping when the final effect set is fixed. [URP graphics settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/urp-global-settings.html)
- Unity 6.6 URP's Gaussian depth-of-field shader applies perspective-only `LinearEyeDepth` to an orthographic camera. That maps Topaz's visible depths to near-plane values, making the blur controls ineffective. `OrthographicGaussianDepthOfField.shader` corrects only the circle-of-confusion depth conversion using URP's `LinearDepthToEyeDepth` helper; ShaderLab `UsePass` pulls the four blur/composite passes from Unity's own shader. `OrthographicPostProcessData.asset` overrides only that shader reference and retains URP's other post-processing resources. Review this small adapter on Unity upgrades. [ShaderLab UsePass](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/SL-UsePass.html)
- Bokeh mode uses the same limited orthographic depth correction and Unity's four built-in Bokeh blur/composite passes. The custom CoC pass adds sub-LSB spatial dither because URP stores the Bokeh blur mask in an 8-bit texture; this breaks up coherent horizontal quantization bands. Its 42-sample blur remains a desktop visual preference whose frame cost needs measurement in a representative scene. [Unity Bokeh controls](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/depth-of-field-volume-override-reference.html)
- The existing URP SSAO renderer feature now has an on/off control. Its stronger comparison settings are intensity **0.85**, radius **0.25**, half-resolution, four samples, medium blur, and direct-light strength **0.4**. [Unity SSAO settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/ssao-renderer-feature-reference.html)
- The ground-wear projectors, authored decal assets, and desktop Decal Renderer Feature were removed after the ground-art review.

## Effects to audition later

Unity Shader Graph includes **Parallax Mapping** for heightmapped material surfaces. That is separate from scrolling background layers. The current orthographic 3D world already has authored geometry for depth; uniform camera translation offers little natural distance-dependent parallax. Reserve layered scrolling for a specific backdrop, and material parallax for a surface whose detail needs it. [Shader Graph parallax](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.shadergraph@17.0/manual/Parallax-Mapping-Node.html)

Judge Bokeh and AO comparisons in motion and on later authored terrain before choosing shipping defaults. Avoid global motion blur during action.

Earlier Mac player checks showed that the 24/30/1 focus preview visibly softened the distant skeleton and rocks while the player stayed sharp. The shader compiled in Mac and Windows builds; the Mac player logged no shader errors. A faint horizontal transition can still occur over the broad flat ground where fog or depth-of-field strength begins; recheck that appearance when terrain and authored scenery replace the test plane.

The scene remains a small art audition. Judge each setting in motion at 60 Hz on Mac, including aim, dodge, combat tells, and foliage edges. Formal Windows 1080p/120 FPS conclusions require a representative standalone scene on the designated PC. See [performance process](../PERFORMANCE.md).
