# URP Visual Lab

The Bootstrap Mac build contains a reversible visual study for the KayKit art. Open **Options → Visual Lab** from the title or Esc pause menu, or press **F7** during play. F5 cycles Lighting only, Painterly, and Focus preview; F6 cycles anti-aliasing Off, FXAA, SMAA, and TAA. These are review controls, not final game settings.

The menu changes 14 values live: color temperature, exposure, contrast, saturation, bloom strength and threshold, vignette, three focus controls, fog end distance, home light intensity, shadow strength, and the extra warmth near home. **Focus mode** switches Gaussian and Bokeh; the three focus sliders change labels and ranges to match the selected mode. Gaussian start/end stay ordered when either slider moves. **Ambient occlusion** and **Ground detail** each have an on/off comparison button. The Look, AA, and Depth of field buttons provide the same comparisons as the shortcuts. **Reset** restores authored study values. **Save selection** writes `visual-study-settings.json` in Unity's persistent data directory and reloads it on the next Mac launch; it is separate from the gameplay save. Existing version-1 selections migrate with the new controls at their defaults. **Copy values** places that JSON on the clipboard. Editor tests use isolated temporary settings paths.

## Current review defaults

The owner's current numerical choice is color temperature **25**, exposure **0**, contrast **0**, saturation **10**, bloom strength **1.00** with threshold **0.70**, vignette **0**, home light **10**, home warmth **35**, shadow strength **0.90**, **TAA**, and depth of field **on**. Unity's positive temperature values add warmth; 25 is less warm than the previous 40, while the home grade adds 35 for an effective 60 near the homestead. Gaussian remains the focus default. The AO and ground-detail comparisons begin **on**. These are study defaults, not established performance targets. Biomes may later have separate color temperature and atmosphere profiles.

For Gaussian depth of field, **Start 24** means distant pixels begin to blur at 24 world units from the camera, **End 30** means the blur reaches its maximum at 30, and **Radius 1.0** sets that maximum blur size. In the current balanced camera, the player is about 22 units away and the visible ground spans roughly 14–33 units. This makes the far-field softening easier to see while keeping the player clear. Unity warns that radius values above 1 can produce undersampling artifacts. [Depth-of-field reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/depth-of-field-volume-override-reference.html)

The scene uses linear fog beginning at **26** world units. **Fog End 65** means the fog color would be fully applied at 65; a smaller end value strengthens haze in the visible far field, while a larger one weakens it. It is intentionally subtle at the current camera scale. [Unity fog start](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/ScriptReference/RenderSettings-fogStartDistance.html) · [fog end](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/ScriptReference/RenderSettings-fogEndDistance.html)

## Native Unity setup

- The [URP Volume framework](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/volumes-landing-page.html) uses `Painterly Clear` globally, `Warm Home` locally with the player as the camera's volume trigger, and a `Focus Preview` overlay. Authored profiles live under `Assets/Topaz/VisualStudy/Profiles/`; the player clones them before live slider changes.
- The default grade uses Neutral tonemapping to retain KayKit's gradient-atlas colors, the chosen warm color values, bloom, and Gaussian depth of field. [Bloom reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/post-processing-bloom.html)
- The camera defaults to the owner's selected TAA. It can ghost on motion, so compare it with SMAA in the player. MSAA stays disabled during this comparison; URP documents that TAA cannot be combined with MSAA. [Unity 6.6 URP anti-aliasing](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/anti-aliasing.html)
- The camera's native **Dithering** option is on to reduce 8-bit banding across the floor's broad light and fog gradients. URP exposes this camera setting as on/off; it has no strength or pattern slider. [Unity camera reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/camera-component-reference.html)
- The desktop URP asset uses **64-bit HDR color buffer precision** (RGBA16F) for lighting and post-processing gradients, as requested. Unity notes that this can reduce banding but uses more memory bandwidth and can sample more slowly than 32-bit HDR; compare frame time once a representative scene exists. Final display dithering remains useful because it addresses the later 8-bit output stage. [Unity URP HDR precision](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/universalrp-asset.html)
- Standalone Player Settings use **High Quality lightmap encoding** for Mac and Windows. This governs baked lightmaps when Topaz generates them; the present test scene primarily uses real-time lighting, so this setting is unlikely to alter its current gradients. [Unity macOS Player settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/PlayerSettings-macOS.html)
- A cool real-time directional light supplies soft shadows; one unshadowed warm point light and a local Volume distinguish the homestead. The directional shadow distance is **52** world units with two cascades split at **45%**. The original 32-unit limit placed URP's final shadow fade inside the visible ground, so turning the aim or moving the camera could sweep the fade across the scene. At maximum zoom, the far visible ground is roughly 40 units from the camera. URP starts fading the new 52-unit shadows around 46 units, leaving room for current off-screen casters whose shadows reach the view. Recheck this margin when taller authored scenery arrives. A KayKit lantern has an emissive ember and a bounded built-in Particle System. [Unity URP shadow settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/universalrp-asset.html)
- The review build disables URP's **Strip Unused Post Processing Variants** setting. A headless build classified Gaussian depth of field as inactive and removed its shader, so slider changes had no visible effect. Unity documents this setting as assuming that runtime Volume profiles do not introduce new effects; Topaz clones and changes Volume profiles for the Visual Lab. Re-evaluate targeted stripping when the final effect set is fixed. [URP graphics settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/urp-global-settings.html)
- Unity 6.6 URP's Gaussian depth-of-field shader applies perspective-only `LinearEyeDepth` to an orthographic camera. That maps Topaz's visible depths to near-plane values, making the blur controls ineffective. `OrthographicGaussianDepthOfField.shader` corrects only the circle-of-confusion depth conversion using URP's `LinearDepthToEyeDepth` helper; ShaderLab `UsePass` pulls the four blur/composite passes from Unity's own shader. `OrthographicPostProcessData.asset` overrides only that shader reference and retains URP's other post-processing resources. Review this small adapter on Unity upgrades. [ShaderLab UsePass](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/SL-UsePass.html)
- Bokeh mode uses the same limited orthographic depth correction and Unity's four built-in Bokeh blur/composite passes. Its comparison defaults are **focus distance 22**, **aperture 1.25**, and **focal length 145 mm**. A lower aperture or longer focal length gives stronger blur; Bokeh also softens close foreground objects. The custom CoC pass adds sub-LSB spatial dither because URP stores the Bokeh blur mask in an 8-bit texture; this breaks up coherent horizontal quantization bands. Its 42-sample blur is a desktop visual study, and its frame cost still needs measurement in a representative scene. [Unity Bokeh controls](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/depth-of-field-volume-override-reference.html)
- The existing URP SSAO renderer feature now has an on/off control. Its stronger comparison settings are intensity **0.85**, radius **0.25**, half-resolution, four samples, medium blur, and direct-light strength **0.4**. [Unity SSAO settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/ssao-renderer-feature-reference.html)
- Three authored ground-wear patches use Unity's Decal Projector and the URP screen-space Decal Renderer Feature. Their one small, project-generated texture and one instanced material are under `Assets/Topaz/VisualStudy/Decals/`; projector draw distance is capped at **45**. The alpha and projector fade were raised after the first build made them barely perceptible. The Ground detail button turns off both their objects and renderer feature. [URP decals](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/renderer-feature-decal.html)

## Effects to audition later

Unity Shader Graph includes **Parallax Mapping** for heightmapped material surfaces. That is separate from scrolling background layers. The current orthographic 3D world already has authored geometry for depth; uniform camera translation offers little natural distance-dependent parallax. Reserve layered scrolling for a specific backdrop, and material parallax for a surface whose detail needs it. [Shader Graph parallax](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.shadergraph@17.0/manual/Parallax-Mapping-Node.html)

Judge the new Bokeh, decal, and AO comparisons in motion and on later authored terrain before choosing shipping defaults. Avoid global motion blur during action.

In the 2026-09-23 Mac player check, toggling F5 between Painterly and Focus visibly softened the distant skeleton and rocks while the player stayed sharp. The shader compiled in Mac and Windows builds; the Mac player logged no shader errors. A faint horizontal transition can still occur over the broad flat ground where fog or depth-of-field strength begins; recheck that appearance when terrain and authored scenery replace the test plane.

The scene remains a small art audition. Judge each setting in motion at 60 Hz on Mac, including aim, dodge, combat tells, and foliage edges. Formal Windows 1080p/120 FPS conclusions require a representative standalone scene on the designated PC. See [performance process](PERFORMANCE.md).
